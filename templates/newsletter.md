# Newsletter Template

## Specifications

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Body Length | 500-900 words |
| Subject Line | Under 50 characters |
| Preview Text | 40-90 characters, different from subject |
| Story Requirement | 1 story from story bank, told fully |
| CTA | Single, clear, low-friction |
| Tone | Personal. Written to one person, not an audience. |
| Frequency | 2 per week |
| Format | Plain text preferred. Minimal HTML. No heavy design templates. |

---

## Newsletter Structure

```
Subject: [Under 50 characters. Curiosity or benefit-driven.]
Preview: [40-90 characters. Adds context. Not a repeat of subject.]

---

[PERSONAL OPENING]
2-3 sentences. Conversational. Sets the scene or establishes the mood.
Can be a quick personal aside, a situational detail, or a direct
address to the reader. This is where personality lives.

[THE STORY]
200-400 words. One story from the story bank, told fully. This is
not a summary or a reference. This is the story told with detail,
tension, and emotional texture. The reader should feel like they
were there.

[THE LESSON]
100-200 words. What the story means. What the reader should take
from it. This is where the insight lives. Keep it focused -- one
lesson, not three.

[BRIDGE TO CTA]
1-2 sentences. Connects the lesson to the action you want the
reader to take. This should feel natural, not forced.

[CTA]
1-3 sentences. Single, clear, low-friction. The reader knows
exactly what to do and why.

[SIGN-OFF]
Personal, warm, consistent. The same sign-off every time builds
familiarity.

[NAME]
[Optional P.S. -- highly read section, good for secondary CTA or
personal aside]
```

## Subject Line Patterns

### Curiosity-Driven (Mueller, Buchan DNA)
- "The call I almost didn't answer"
- "What nobody told me about [topic]"
- "I made a $87K mistake"
- "This changes everything"
- "The story I've never told"
- "Something weird happened last week"
- "[Name] was wrong about this"

### Benefit-Driven (Hormozi, Lead Gen Jay DNA)
- "The [number]-step fix for [problem]"
- "How I [achieved result] in [timeframe]"
- "Steal this [framework/process/template]"
- "The one thing that actually works"
- "[Specific result] without [sacrifice]"

### Direct/Personal (Brian Kurtz, Bilyeu DNA)
- "A question for you"
- "I need to tell you something"
- "The lesson that took me [time] to learn"
- "Read this before [day/event]"
- "Quick update (and a gift)"

### Rules for Subject Lines
- Under 50 characters (40 is ideal for mobile preview)
- No ALL CAPS
- No excessive punctuation (one question mark or exclamation max)
- No spam trigger words: "free", "guaranteed", "act now", "limited time", "congratulations"
- Test: would you open this email from a friend? If not, rewrite it.
- Do not reveal the entire point in the subject line. Curiosity drives opens.

### Preview Text Rules
- 40-90 characters
- Adds information the subject line does not contain
- Never repeats or summarizes the subject line
- Can be a continuation: Subject "I made a $87K mistake" / Preview "And it happened because I ignored one sentence."
- Can be a teaser: Subject "The one thing that actually works" / Preview "Hint: it's not what LinkedIn tells you."
- Can be a direct address: Subject "Quick question" / Preview "About your hiring process."

## Opening Patterns

### The Scene-Setter (Mueller DNA)

Drop the reader into a moment. Make it feel intimate and present.

```
Last Tuesday I was sitting in my car in the parking lot after a meeting
that went sideways. I had 14 missed calls and a text from my business
partner that just said "call me."

Here's what happened.
```

### The Direct Address (Buchan, Brian Kurtz DNA)

Talk to the reader like a friend. Acknowledge something happening in their world or yours.

```
Can I be honest with you for a second?

I almost didn't write this email. The story I'm about to tell you is
one I've been avoiding for two years. But you deserve to hear it.
```

### The Quick Hit (Hormozi, Lead Gen Jay DNA)

Get to the value immediately. No warm-up.

```
I found the bottleneck.

After looking at 23 different [businesses/pipelines/teams] this quarter,
every single one had the same problem. And the fix took less than a week.
```

### The Aside (Buchan DNA)

Start with something unexpected or personal that connects to the main topic sideways.

```
My daughter asked me yesterday why I stare at my phone so much during dinner.

I gave her some corporate answer about "being available for clients."
She said: "But you're not available for us."

That seven-year-old just summed up the problem with how most of us
run our businesses.
```

## Story-Telling Rules for Newsletters

Newsletters are where you tell stories fully. Not summarized. Not referenced. TOLD.

### Story Arc for Newsletter

1. **Set the scene** (2-3 sentences): Where were you? When? What was the context? Ground the reader in a specific moment.
2. **Introduce the tension** (2-3 sentences): What went wrong? What was at stake? What was the decision point?
3. **Build the middle** (3-5 sentences): What happened? What did you do? What surprised you? Include specific details -- the exact words someone said, the specific number on the screen, the feeling in your gut.
4. **The turn** (1-2 sentences): The moment everything changed. The realization, the decision, the unexpected outcome.
5. **The resolution** (2-3 sentences): What happened as a result. Specific outcome. What is different now.

### Detail Rules
- Use dialogue when the story bank provides it. Direct quotes make stories feel real.
- Include sensory details when available. "I was sitting in my car in the parking lot" beats "After the meeting."
- Include specific numbers. "$87K" beats "a lot of money." "3 months" beats "a few months."
- Include the emotional beat. "My stomach dropped" or "I couldn't stop grinning" -- the client's actual emotional response.

---

## Lesson Section Rules

The lesson is short. The story does the heavy lifting.

- **One lesson per newsletter.** Not three things I learned. One thing.
- **State the lesson in one sentence.** Bold it. This is the tweet-able takeaway.
- **Expand with 2-3 sentences** of context, nuance, or application.
- **Connect to the reader's world.** "You've probably felt this too" or "If you're in [situation], this might be what's happening."

Do not:
- List multiple takeaways
- Turn the lesson into a sermon
- Add caveats that water down the point ("Of course, this doesn't apply to everyone...")
- Repeat the story in summary form

## CTA Patterns for Newsletters

### Reply CTAs (Highest Engagement)
- "Hit reply and tell me: have you been through something like this?"
- "I'm genuinely curious -- what would you have done in my shoes? Reply and tell me."
- "Reply with one word that describes your [current situation with topic]."

### Click CTAs (Lead Generation)
- "I put the full framework in a one-page PDF. [Grab it here]."
- "If you want help with [specific thing], [this is a good next step]."
- "I recorded a 12-minute breakdown of this. [Watch it here]."

### Share CTAs (Growth)
- "If you know someone who needs to hear this, forward this email to them."
- "Know a [specific role] who is dealing with [specific problem]? Send this their way."

### Soft CTAs (Relationship Building)
- "That's it for today. Talk next [day]."
- "More on this Thursday. For now, just sit with the question."

## Sign-Off Format

Consistent sign-off across all newsletters. Choose one and stick with it:

```
Talk soon,
[FIRST NAME]
```

```
Rooting for you,
[FIRST NAME]
```

```
-- [FIRST NAME]
```

```
Until next time,
[FIRST NAME]
```

Pick the sign-off that matches the client's voice. Casual clients: "Talk soon" or just their name. Warmer clients: "Rooting for you." Professional clients: keep it to the name with a dash.

## P.S. Section (Optional but Powerful)

The P.S. is one of the most-read parts of any email. Use it strategically.

**For secondary CTAs**:
```
P.S. I'm opening 5 spots for [offer] next month. If you want first access,
reply "interested" and I'll send you the details before I announce publicly.
```

**For personal asides**:
```
P.S. My daughter now charges me $1 every time she catches me on my phone
at dinner. I owe her $14 so far this week. She's learning entrepreneurship
faster than I did.
```

**For social proof**:
```
P.S. Last week's email about [topic] got 47 replies. The best response came
from [first name], who said [brief quote]. You all are way smarter than me.
```

**Rules for P.S.**:
- Max 3 sentences
- Must feel like an afterthought, not a planned section
- Do not use P.S. in every newsletter -- 50-60% frequency keeps it feeling authentic
- Never make the P.S. more important than the main CTA
