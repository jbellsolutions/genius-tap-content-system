# Email Nurture Sequence Template

## Specifications

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Sequence Length | 5-7 emails |
| Per-Email Length | 200-500 words |
| Subject Line | Under 45 characters |
| Day Spacing | 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10 |
| Format | Plain text preferred (no heavy HTML) |
| CTA Per Email | Single. Escalates from low-friction to high-friction. |
| Story Integration | At least 3 stories woven across the sequence |
| Spam Trigger Words | None in subject lines or body |

---

## Sequence Architecture

The nurture sequence is a narrative arc spread across 5-7 emails. Each email must work as a standalone piece (someone might open email 4 without reading 1-3), but together they build escalating trust and commitment.

### 7-Email Sequence Arc

```
EMAIL 1 (Day 0): WELCOME + POSITIONING
  Purpose: Introduce the client, establish credibility, set expectations
  CTA: Read/consume (lowest friction)
  DNA: Mueller (story) or Brian Kurtz (authority)
  Story: Origin story or defining-moment story

EMAIL 2 (Day 1): PROBLEM AGITATION
  Purpose: Show deep understanding of the reader's pain
  CTA: Read/reflect (low friction)
  DNA: Mueller (empathy through story) or Bilyeu (identity-level framing)
  Story: Story where the client experienced the same problem the reader has

EMAIL 3 (Day 2): MECHANISM REVEAL
  Purpose: Introduce the client's unique framework/approach
  CTA: Click (medium friction -- link to resource, video, or article)
  DNA: Todd Brown (mechanism) or Jay Abraham (strategic reframe)
  Story: Story of discovering or developing the framework

EMAIL 4 (Day 3): PROOF
  Purpose: Show the framework working with specific results
  CTA: Click (medium friction -- case study, calculator, or assessment)
  DNA: Hormozi (math/results) or Lead Gen Jay (specific process + results)
  Story: Client success story with concrete numbers

EMAIL 5 (Day 5): OBJECTION HANDLING
  Purpose: Address the biggest "but what about..." objections
  CTA: Reply (medium-high friction -- encourages conversation)
  DNA: Hormozi (contrarian reframe) or Buchan (disarming honesty)
  Story: Story of a client/customer who had the same objection and what happened

EMAIL 6 (Day 7): VALUE STACK
  Purpose: Show everything they get, make the offer feel like a no-brainer
  CTA: Buy/book (high friction)
  DNA: Hormozi (value equation) or Lead Gen Jay (full breakdown)
  Story: Before/after transformation story

EMAIL 7 (Day 10): FINAL PUSH
  Purpose: Create urgency without sleaze, make the direct ask
  CTA: Buy/book (highest friction, most direct)
  DNA: Brian Kurtz (authority + urgency) or Hormozi (direct)
  Story: Story of someone who almost did not take action and what happened
```

### 5-Email Compressed Sequence

For shorter campaigns, merge emails 2+3 and emails 5+6:

```
EMAIL 1 (Day 0): Welcome + Positioning
EMAIL 2 (Day 1): Problem + Mechanism (agitate then reveal the solution framework)
EMAIL 3 (Day 3): Proof (results story with specific numbers)
EMAIL 4 (Day 5): Objection Handling + Value Stack (address fear, then stack the value)
EMAIL 5 (Day 8): Final Push (urgency + direct ask)
```

## Email-by-Email Templates

### Email 1: Welcome + Positioning (Day 0)

```
Subject: [Under 45 chars. Personal, sets the tone.]
         Examples: "Here's what you signed up for"
                   "Quick story about how this started"
                   "Two things you should know"

---

[GREETING: Personal, warm, brief]
Hey [FIRST_NAME],

[POSITIONING STATEMENT: 2-3 sentences. Who you are, what you do,
why the reader should care. Not a bio. A value statement.]

[ORIGIN STORY: 3-5 sentences. The short version of how the client
got to where they are. Focus on the turning point, not the resume.
Use specific details from the story bank.]

[EXPECTATION SETTING: 2-3 sentences. What the reader will get from
these emails. How often. What to expect.]

[CTA: Low-friction. Read, watch, or simply reply.]
If you want to know the one thing that changed everything for me,
I'm going to share it tomorrow.

For now -- glad you're here.

[SIGN-OFF]
[NAME]

P.S. [Optional. A quick personal detail or a "reply and tell me
about yourself" prompt to start a conversation.]
```

### Email 2: Problem Agitation (Day 1)

```
Subject: [Names the problem. Under 45 chars.]
         Examples: "The real reason it's not working"
                   "Nobody talks about this part"
                   "Sound familiar?"

---

[HOOK: Open with a scenario the reader is living. Be specific.
Not "Are you struggling with X?" but paint the exact scene.]

[AGITATION: 3-5 sentences. Go deeper into the pain. Name the
specific frustrations, the failed attempts, the self-doubt.
Show you've been there.]

[STORY: 3-5 sentences from the story bank. A time when the client
was in the same position as the reader. Specific details. The reader
should think "this person actually understands."]

[BRIDGE: 1-2 sentences. Hint that there's a different way. Don't
reveal it yet. Build anticipation for email 3.]

[CTA: Read/reflect.]
Tomorrow I'm going to share the single insight that changed
everything. It's not what you'd expect.

[SIGN-OFF]
```

### Email 3: Mechanism Reveal (Day 2)

```
Subject: [Teases the mechanism. Under 45 chars.]
         Examples: "The thing nobody's telling you"
                   "Here's why [common approach] fails"
                   "I found the bottleneck"

---

[CALLBACK: 1 sentence referencing yesterday's email. Not a summary.
A continuation.]

[MECHANISM SETUP: 2-3 sentences. Name the common approach to the
problem. Acknowledge why it makes sense on the surface.]

[THE REVEAL: 3-5 sentences. Introduce the client's unique framework,
approach, or insight. Name it if it has a name. Explain WHY the
common approach fails and WHY this approach works. Use Todd Brown
DNA: mechanism-focused, educational, building curiosity.]

[STORY: 3-5 sentences. How the client discovered or developed this
approach. Specific moment of realization. From the story bank.]

[PROOF TEASE: 1-2 sentences. Hint at results. Build anticipation
for email 4.]

[CTA: Medium friction. Link to a resource.]
I put together a [short guide / video / worksheet] that breaks this
down step by step. [Link]

[SIGN-OFF]
```

### Email 4: Proof (Day 3)

```
Subject: [Results-focused. Under 45 chars.]
         Examples: "$52K to $298K in 13 months"
                   "The numbers speak"
                   "Here's what happened next"

---

[HOOK: Lead with the result. Specific number, specific timeframe.]

[CASE STUDY STORY: 5-8 sentences. A full story from the story bank
about a client or the client themselves achieving specific results.
Include: starting point, the problem, the approach used (the
mechanism from email 3), the outcome, the timeline.]

[BREAKDOWN: 2-3 sentences. The math or logic behind why it worked.
Hormozi DNA: make the result feel logical, not miraculous.]

[GENERALIZATION: 2-3 sentences. Connect the specific case study to
the reader's situation. Not "you can do this too" generic. More:
"If you're in [specific situation], the first step would be [specific
action]."]

[CTA: Medium friction.]
Want to see if this would work for your [business/situation]?
[Link to assessment / calendar / resource]

[SIGN-OFF]
```

### Email 5: Objection Handling (Day 5)

```
Subject: [Addresses the unspoken concern. Under 45 chars.]
         Examples: "The question everyone asks"
                   "But what if it doesn't work?"
                   "Let me address the elephant"

---

[ACKNOWLEDGE THE OBJECTION: 1-2 sentences. Name the biggest concern
the reader likely has. Be honest and direct. Not "I know you might
be thinking..." but more "Here's the concern I hear most often:"]

[STORY: 4-6 sentences. Story from the story bank about someone who
had the exact same objection. What happened when they acted despite
the concern. Or what happened when they did NOT act because of it.]

[REFRAME: 2-3 sentences. Flip the objection. The risk of NOT acting
is greater than the risk of acting. Use Hormozi DNA: math-based or
logic-based reframe.]

[SECONDARY OBJECTION: 2-3 sentences. Address one more common concern
briefly. Keep it tight.]

[CTA: Medium-high friction. Reply.]
If you have a question I haven't covered, reply to this email and
ask me directly. I read every response.

[SIGN-OFF]
```

### Email 6: Value Stack (Day 7)

```
Subject: [Summarizes the offer value. Under 45 chars.]
         Examples: "Everything you get"
                   "The full picture"
                   "Here's what I'd do for you"

---

[RESTATE THE PROBLEM: 1-2 sentences. Remind the reader what they're
dealing with. Callback to email 2.]

[RESTATE THE MECHANISM: 1-2 sentences. Remind them of the approach.
Callback to email 3.]

[RESTATE THE PROOF: 1-2 sentences. Remind them it works. Callback
to email 4.]

[VALUE STACK: The full offer breakdown. Use Hormozi DNA.]

Here's everything you get:

- [Component 1]: [What it is] (valued at $[X])
- [Component 2]: [What it is] (valued at $[X])
- [Component 3]: [What it is] (valued at $[X])
- [Bonus 1]: [What it is] (valued at $[X])
- [Bonus 2]: [What it is] (valued at $[X])

Total value: $[SUM]
Your investment: $[PRICE]

[BEFORE/AFTER: 3-4 sentences. Paint the picture of life after. Not
vague "imagine the possibilities." Specific: "Six months from now,
you'll have [specific outcome], [specific outcome], and [specific
outcome]. And you'll wonder why you waited."]

[CTA: High friction. Buy/book.]
[Clear link or action step]

[SIGN-OFF]
```

### Email 7: Final Push (Day 10)

```
Subject: [Direct, urgent without sleaze. Under 45 chars.]
         Examples: "Last chance (genuinely)"
                   "Quick question before I close this"
                   "Are you in or out?"
                   "One more thing, then I'll stop"

---

[DIRECT ADDRESS: 1-2 sentences. Acknowledge this is the last email
in the sequence. Be transparent about it.]

[STORY: 3-5 sentences. Story about someone who almost did not take
action. What would have happened if they hadn't. Or: story about
someone who did NOT take action and the cost.]

[THE ASK: 2-3 sentences. Direct. No tricks. "I'd love to work with
you. If this is the right fit, here's how to take the next step.
If it's not, no hard feelings."]

[URGENCY (if real): 1-2 sentences. Only if there is genuine scarcity
or a deadline. Never manufacture fake urgency.]

[CTA: Highest friction. Direct buy/book.]
[Link or action step]

[CLOSING: 2-3 sentences. Warm, genuine, no guilt. Express that you
will continue sending value regardless of whether they buy.]

[SIGN-OFF]

P.S. [Final push. Quick, punchy. Either a deadline reminder or a
restatement of the core transformation.]
```

## Subject Line Rules for Email Sequences

### Progression
Subject lines should escalate in directness across the sequence:
- Email 1: Warm, personal ("Here's what you signed up for")
- Email 2-3: Curious, educational ("The real reason it's not working")
- Email 4-5: Evidence-based, direct ("$52K to $298K in 13 months")
- Email 6-7: Urgent, action-oriented ("Are you in?")

### Spam Avoidance
Never use in subject lines:
- "FREE" (all caps)
- "Act now"
- "Limited time"
- "Congratulations"
- "You've been selected"
- "Guaranteed"
- "No obligation"
- Multiple exclamation marks
- All caps for any word
- Dollar signs ($) in subject line (OK in body)
- "Click here" or "Open now"

## Sequence-Level Quality Checks

Before submitting the sequence to the Critic:

1. **Read all emails in order.** Does the narrative arc make sense? Does each email build on the last?
2. **Read email 4 alone.** Does it stand on its own? Someone might open just this one.
3. **Check story variety.** Are you using at least 3 different stories across the sequence?
4. **Check CTA escalation.** Does the ask get progressively bigger? Never ask for a purchase in email 1.
5. **Check tone consistency.** Does every email sound like the same person wrote it?
6. **Check length.** Every email should be 200-500 words. If one is 800 words, cut it or split it.
7. **Check for repetition.** The same point should not be made the same way in two different emails. You can revisit themes but from new angles.
