# Swipe File Context

## What Is the Swipe File?

The Genius Tap swipe file is a curated library of **5,716 real emails** from **9 master copywriters**. These are not hypothetical examples or AI-generated samples. They are actual emails sent to real audiences by copywriters who have collectively generated hundreds of millions of dollars in revenue.

The swipe file serves one purpose in this system: it provides **structural DNA** for content creation. We do not copy words, phrases, or ideas from the swipe file. We extract **patterns** -- hook structures, story architectures, transition mechanics, CTA frameworks, and tonal rhythms -- and apply those patterns to the client's own voice, stories, and expertise.

The swipe file is located at `../Real Swipe File/data/` relative to this repo, organized by author.

## The 9 Copywriter Profiles

### 1. Jon Buchan
- **Email Count:** 2,274
- **Primary Style:** Playful / Pattern-Interrupt
- **Signature Patterns:** Humor hooks that disarm the reader before the pitch arrives. Self-deprecating openings that build instant likability. Absurd analogies that make the reader laugh and remember. Fourth-wall breaks that acknowledge the reader knows they are being sold to. Conversational tone that reads like a text from a witty friend, not a marketer.
- **Best Content Types:** Cold emails, ice-breaker LinkedIn posts, connection request follow-ups, newsletter openers, any content where the goal is to get a reply from a stranger.
- **Voice Traits:** High energy, low formality, high humor, medium storytelling, medium contrarian edge.
- **When to Assign:** Client has natural wit or playfulness. Client targets audiences tired of corporate-speak. Client needs to break through inbox noise. Client's brand can handle irreverence.

### 2. Todd Brown

Structural pattern of "Here is why what you have been told is wrong, and here is the mechanism that actually works."
- **Best Content Types:** Course and info-product launches, consultant authority posts, educational LinkedIn articles, lead magnets, webinar promotion emails, any content that needs to position a framework.
- **Voice Traits:** Medium energy, medium-high formality, low humor, high storytelling (through frameworks), high contrarian edge.
- **When to Assign:** Client has a proprietary methodology. Client sells knowledge-based services. Client's audience values expertise and proof. Client needs to differentiate their approach from commodity advice.

## How Agents Use the Swipe File

### The Query Process

When the Style Matcher agent needs to generate a structural blueprint for a content piece, it follows this sequence:

1. **Identify the content type** (LinkedIn post, article, newsletter, cold email, etc.)
2. **Check the client's assigned DNA styles** from the CLAUDE.md client identity block
3. **Query the swipe file by copywriter** to find examples that match the content type
4. **Extract the structural pattern** from 2-3 matching examples (hook type, body architecture, transition mechanics, CTA framework)
5. **Document the pattern** in the structural blueprint for the Creator agent
6. **Note the swipe file references** so the pattern extraction is traceable

### Example Query Flow

Content type: LinkedIn post (authority/contrarian)
Client DNA: Primary Hormozi, Secondary Mueller

1. Pull 3 Hormozi examples that are contrarian/authority posts
2. Extract pattern: "Bold contrarian claim in line 1 > mathematical proof or specific example > reframe that makes the reader question their assumption > direct CTA or conversation starter"
3. Pull 1 Mueller example for story integration
4. Extract pattern: "Scene-setting in 2 sentences > tension or unexpected turn > lesson that connects to the contrarian claim"
5. Combine into a hybrid blueprint: Hormozi structure for the overall architecture, Mueller influence for the story anchor in the middle

### What Agents Extract vs. What They Ignore

**Extract (structural DNA):**
- Hook mechanics (how does the first line grab attention?)
- Story architecture (how does the narrative unfold?)
- Transition patterns (how does the writer move between ideas?)
- CTA frameworks (how does the piece end and what does it ask the reader to do?)
- Tonal rhythm (short sentence then long sentence, question then answer, etc.)
- Engagement triggers (what makes the reader want to keep reading?)

**Ignore (never copy):**
- Specific words or phrases
- Topic-specific content
- Industry-specific examples
- Personal stories or anecdotes from the copywriter
- Product-specific offers or CTAs
- Any copyrighted text

## The DNA Matching Process

When a new client is onboarded, the Style Matcher runs a DNA matching analysis:

### Step 1: Analyze Client Voice

Using the voice profile, score the client on five dimensions:
- **Energy:** Low (measured, deliberate) to High (punchy, rapid-fire)
- **Formality:** Low (casual, colloquial) to High (professional, polished)
- **Humor:** None to High (frequent, integral to voice)
- **Storytelling:** Low (tactical, data-driven) to High (narrative-driven, emotional)
- **Contrarian Edge:** Low (consensus-building) to High (challenges assumptions constantly)

### Step 2: Score Against Each Copywriter

Compare the client's five-dimension score against each copywriter's voice trait profile (defined in `swipe-file/config/authors.json`). Calculate a fit score based on alignment across all five dimensions.

### Step 3: Assign Primary and Secondary Styles

- **Primary DNA (1-2 styles):** Highest fit score. Used for the majority of content. Provides the default structural architecture.
- **Secondary DNA (1 style):** Complementary style that fills a gap. If the primary is high-energy and contrarian (Hormozi), the secondary might be story-driven (Mueller) for content that needs emotional depth.

### Step 4: Map DNA to Content Types

Not every DNA style works for every content type. The mapping considers both the client's voice AND the content format:

| Content Type | Recommended DNA | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn posts (authority) | Hormozi, Jay Abraham | Punchy, contrarian, positions expertise |
| LinkedIn posts (story) | Bill Mueller, Tom Bilyeu | Emotional arc, curiosity hooks |
| LinkedIn posts (tactical) | Lead Gen Jay, Todd Brown | Specificity, frameworks |
| Articles | Bill Mueller, Brian Kurtz | Long-form story capacity, authority |
| Newsletters | Jon Buchan, Brian Kurtz | Personality-forward, relationship-building |
| Cold outreach | Jon Buchan, Hormozi | Pattern-interrupt, value-forward |
| Social captions | Hormozi, Liam Ottley | Punchy, single-idea, scroll-stopping |

Override these defaults based on the client's voice match. A naturally funny client should use Buchan DNA more broadly. A data-driven client should lean Lead Gen Jay across formats.

### Step 5: Document in CLAUDE.md

The final DNA assignment is recorded in the client identity block of CLAUDE.md:

```yaml
PRIMARY_DNA_STYLES:
  - "Alex Hormozi"
  - "Bill Mueller"
SECONDARY_DNA_STYLE: "Jon Buchan"
```

This assignment guides every content generation run until it is updated during a monthly optimization call.
